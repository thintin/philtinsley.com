Registered is not the same as ready: the mobilisation gap for new providers

Phil Tinsley · November 2026

What registration actually tells you

Getting through CQC registration is a real achievement. The application, the policies, the fit person checks and the registration assessment all take time and effort. But it is worth being clear about what registration confirms. It shows CQC is satisfied that your systems, policies and people look right on paper and that you are ready to start. It does not show that those systems work once you are delivering care to real people, with real staff, on real rotas.

That second test comes later, and it arrives whether you are prepared for it or not.

The gap nobody plans for

For many new providers, especially in domiciliary care, there is a gap between registration and steady work. You may need to get onto a local authority framework or approved provider list, and each commissioner sets its own requirements and timetable. Private referrals can be slow to build. In the meantime, the service is registered but delivering little or no care.

This gap matters for regulatory reasons too. If you register a regulated activity and do not start it, or stop for a period, CQC expects you to notify it that the activity is dormant. CQC then asks you every three months to confirm that it remains dormant, and the form has to come back within 14 days. Under the Health and Social Care Act 2008, CQC can also move to cancel a registration where a regulated activity has not been carried on for a continuous 12 months. Dormancy is not a quiet pause. It carries its own obligations.

Use mobilisation to build evidence, not just paperwork

The most useful thing a new provider can do in this period is treat mobilisation as the time to make systems live, not just documented. That means:

  • Training records that are complete, dated and linked to each role, not a folder of certificates
  • Audit tools you have actually run, even on a small scale, so you know how long they take and what they show
  • A governance meeting rhythm with minutes and actions, started before the first package begins
  • Recruitment files you would be comfortable handing to an inspector on day one

Local authority contract monitoring and CQC assessment ask overlapping questions. Both want to see safe recruitment, trained staff, oversight of care delivery and a clear audit trail. If you build one set of records that serves both, you avoid the common trap of running two separate compliance exercises once the work starts.

The first packages are the real test

When referrals do arrive, they often come quickly and with little notice. That is when untested systems show their weaknesses: a rota process nobody has used under pressure, a care plan template that does not fit the first person you support, an audit that has never been completed. Every one of those is easier to fix with no care being delivered than with three new packages starting in the same week.

Registration gets you permission to operate. Mobilisation is where you prove you are ready to.

If your first five packages started next Monday, which part of your system would you be least confident showing to a commissioner or an inspector?
