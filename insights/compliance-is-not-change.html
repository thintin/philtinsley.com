100% compliance can still change nothing

Phil Tinsley · October 2026

A digital rollout can hit 100% compliance and still change nothing. Every carer logs in, every visit is recorded, every care plan is updated on time. The dashboard is green. Yet the people receiving care may be getting exactly the same support they got before the system arrived.

That is not an argument against digital systems. It is an argument about how we judge them.

What usage figures actually tell you

Most digital projects in care get judged on adoption and efficiency: log-in rates, completed fields, time saved on paperwork, fewer missing signatures. All of it is useful. None of it is really the point.

Usage figures tell you whether people used the system. They do not tell you whether the system changed anything. A care plan updated on time but never read by the person delivering care is still just admin. It has moved from a folder to a screen, but nobody acts differently because of it.

It is easy to see why usage figures win. They are available from day one, they are simple to report, and they make a project look finished.

The test that matters is at the sharp end

The question worth asking is simple: what happened at the point of care that would not have happened without this system?

That might be a change in someone’s needs spotted earlier because notes from different visits were visible in one place. It might be a medication issue picked up the same day rather than at the monthly audit. It might be a team leader spending less time chasing paperwork and more time with staff and the people they support.

If nobody can name an outcome like that, the system may be working as software without yet working as care.

Why this matters for governance

Regulation 17 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 requires providers to have systems that assess, monitor and drive improvement in the quality and safety of their services. The word that matters there is improvement. A record that is complete but unused does not drive anything.

The same applies to how you report digital change to your board, your owners or your commissioners. A green adoption dashboard tells them the rollout happened. It does not tell them whether care became safer or more responsive, which is what they need to know.

Signing a system off properly

Before you declare a system as working, agree what outcome you expected it to change, then check whether it did. A few practical questions help:

  • Who reads the information staff record, and what do they do with it?
  • Has anything been spotted sooner, escalated faster or resolved more quickly since go-live?
  • Has any task been removed, or has the system simply added a new one alongside the old?
  • Would staff notice if the system disappeared tomorrow, beyond having one less thing to fill in?

If the honest answers are vague, the project is not finished. It has reached the end of implementation, which is a different thing.

So here is the challenge. Think of the last system you introduced and signed off. Can you name one thing that happened for a person you support, or for a member of your team, that would not have happened without it? If you cannot, what would you need to look at this month to find out?
